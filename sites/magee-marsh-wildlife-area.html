Geologic Age Glossary →
Silurian443-419 Mya

~443 to 419 Mya Silurian dolomite: Tymochtee Dolomite (olive gray to yellowish brown, thin to massive bedded, with carbonaceous shale partings, 0 to 140 feet thick) under the marsh, with the underlying Greenfield Dolomite beneath the boardwalk woods (Ohio Geological Survey 1:24,000 bedrock map, Oak Harbor quadrangle); the bedrock is buried under Late Wisconsinan laminated lacustrine clay laid down in proglacial Lake Maumee, about 14,000 radiocarbon years (about 17,500 calendar years) ago.

Epoch

Silurian (Tymochtee and Greenfield Dolomites); surface Late Wisconsinan glacial lake clay

Native lands Glossary →

Odawa (Ottawa) · Wyandot (Wendat) · Potawatomi (Bodéwadmi) · Erie; the Erie confederacy was destroyed by the Haudenosaunee by 1656 after the war of the mid-1650s; Odawa bands held reserves on the lower Maumee at Roche de Boeuf and Wolf Rapids, at McCarty's village, and a four-miles-square tract on Maumee Bay set aside by the Treaty of Detroit (1807); the Treaty of Greene Ville (August 3 1795) left this shore Indian country, northwest of its boundary line; Wyandot, Seneca, Delaware, Shawnee, Potawatomi, Odawa, and Ojibwe bands ceded nearly all remaining northwestern Ohio, this shore included, at the Treaty of Fort Meigs (Foot of the Rapids), September 29 1817; the Treaty with the Ottawa at Maumee, February 18 1833, ceded the band's remaining lands on both sides of the Maumee and on Maumee Bay; most Maumee valley Odawa were removed west in three journeys in 1832, 1837, and 1839 to the Marais des Cygnes River in Kansas, some fleeing to Walpole Island, and the Ottawa Tribe of Oklahoma records that about half of the roughly 500 who left Ohio died within a few years of illness, malnutrition, and exposure; the Wyandot left Upper Sandusky for Kansas in July 1843, the last removal from Ohio.

Displacement & Tenure

Cession 87: Treaty of Fort Meigs, or Treaty of the Foot of the Rapids (1817); the land lay northwest of the 1795 Greene Ville line and stayed Indian country until 1817. After Black Swamp settlement accelerated in the 1850s the Lake Erie shore marshes were bought up by duck hunters for private clubs and preserves, closed to the public. John Magee acquired 2,700 acres of marsh in 1903 to make farmland, and when Lake Erie kept breaching his dikes he returned it to marsh and private hunting. The State of Ohio bought 1,800 acres of the marsh in 1951 to create the Magee Marsh Wildlife Area, managed by the ODNR Division of Wildlife; Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge opened alongside it in 1961, and the adjoining Crane Creek State Park was closed in 2008 and its acres added to the wildlife area.

Shadow History

The marsh is a remnant of the Great Black Swamp, the glacial wetland of the Maumee basin that was drained for farming chiefly between 1859 and 1885; the agricultural runoff from the farmland that replaced it drives Lake Erie's recurring harmful algal blooms. By 1900 duck hunters had bought the Lake Erie shore marshes for private clubs and preserves that were off limits to the public, and Magee itself was a private hunting marsh until the 1951 state purchase. Davis-Besse Nuclear Power Station, about 4 miles east on the same shore, began commercial operation July 31 1978; 733 acres of its 954-acre site are part of Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge. On March 5 2002, during a shutdown, workers found a cavity in the reactor vessel head where cracked control rod nozzles had leaked borated coolant and boric acid had eaten through the 6-inch carbon steel, leaving only the 3/8-inch stainless steel cladding holding pressure; the NRC estimated the cavity had grown undetected for about 10 years, its Lessons Learned Task Force found the damage preventable, and the agency imposed its largest fine to that date, more than $5 million. The plant restarted in March 2004.

Ecology

Coastal Lake Erie emergent freshwater marsh, held behind dikes, with cattail (Typha spp.), bulrush (Schoenoplectus spp.), arrowhead (Sagittaria latifolia), and pickerelweed (Pontederia cordata), and a strip of wooded beach ridge along the shore; the boardwalk through about seven acres of swamp woods is a premier spring warbler migration site, with 34 to 37 warbler species recorded during the May Biggest Week in American Birding festival.

Hydrology

Lake Erie (southwestern shore, western basin); Crane Creek reaches the lake through the adjoining Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge, and the marsh pools are held behind dikes against high lake levels; the site lies on the lake plain of proglacial Lake Maumee, whose laminated clay forms the impermeable marsh floor; the Maumee River, the largest watershed of any Great Lakes river (US EPA), enters Lake Erie at Toledo about 16 miles west.
