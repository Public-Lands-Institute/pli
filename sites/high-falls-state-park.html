Native lands Glossary →

Muscogee (Mvskoke) confederacy; Hitchiti-speaking Okmulgee towns historically situated between the Towaliga River and Walnut Creek; the river name Towaliga derives from the Itsate-Creek "towalika," a hewn wooden bench; Ocmulgee River corridor was Muscogee heartland until the Yamasee War of 1715 destroyed Okmulgee Town; ceded via Treaty of Indian Springs (1821, lands east of the Flint River) and the second Treaty of Indian Springs (1825), the latter signed by Muscogee leader William McIntosh, who was executed by his own nation months later for the unauthorized cession

Displacement & Tenure

Cession: Treaty of Indian Springs (1821), ceding Creek lands including the future Monroe County (organized May 15, 1821 directly from this cession); reinforced by the second Treaty of Indian Springs (1825), ceding virtually all remaining Muscogee land in Georgia. Site tenure: a gristmill built in 1827 grew under T.S.M. Bloodworth into a mill village with sawmills, cotton gins, and other industry; Confederate forces burned the mill in November 1864, and Bloodworth rebuilt it, but the Panic of 1873 ruined him financially and the village was abandoned. Captain Seaton Grantland's Towaliga Falls Power Company built a hydroelectric dam here, completed in 1904; the property passed to Georgia Hydroelectric Company in 1925, then to Georgia Power Company in 1930, which operated it until 1958. Hiwassee Land Company, a subsidiary of Bowaters Paper Corporation, acquired the property in 1961, timbered it, auctioned off the old gristmill, and donated the lake and surrounding land to the Georgia Game and Fish Commission on July 19, 1961; the commission ran it as an experimental park before transferring it to the State Parks Division, which formally established High Falls State Park in 1966.

Ecology

Piedmont hardwood forest with boulder-strewn riverine bluffs along the Towaliga River gorge; mixed hardwoods and understory wildflowers and fungi typical of the fall-line Piedmont.

Hydrology

Towaliga River (52.3 mi tributary of the Ocmulgee River), Upper Ocmulgee and Altamaha basin; the falls drop about 135 feet over exposed granite and gneiss at the fall line; a dam (completed 1904) impounds the 650-acre High Falls Lake.
