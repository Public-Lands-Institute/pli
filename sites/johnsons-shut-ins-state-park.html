Johnson's Shut-Ins State Park

MO
Geologic Age Glossary →
Proterozoic

~1.47 billion years ago (bedrock).

Epoch

Proterozoic eon; Mesoproterozoic era, Calymmian period; St. Francois Mountains rhyolite and granite among the oldest exposed rock in Missouri; river incision Quaternary.

Native lands Glossary →

Osage Nation (Wazhazhe) · earlier Paleo-Indian peoples; Osage hunted throughout the St. Francois Mountains; ceded territory via 1808 treaty under duress.

Displacement & Tenure

Cession 67: Treaty with the Osage (1808); on December 14, 2005, the Taum Sauk upper reservoir owned by AmerenUE failed, releasing approximately 1.3 billion gallons of water that destroyed the lower park; AmerenUE pled no contest to a federal Clean Water Act violation; a 2007 settlement required Ameren to pay $180 million in cash and property, including about $103 million to clean up and restore the park, completed in 2010.

Shadow History

The park was assembled over 17 years by Joseph Desloge, heir to a St. Louis lead-mining family whose wealth derived from mineral extraction in the same regional belt as the park's Precambrian geology, and donated to the state in 1955; a 2006 to 2007 USGS study following the 2005 reservoir breach documented elevated turbidity, dissolved aluminum, dissolved iron, and a blue-green surface coloration in the East Fork Black River attributed to rock flour and an alum-based flocculant used by AmerenUE; the East Fork Black River corridor has documented Paleo-Indian occupation extending to approximately 12,000 years BP, and whether the flood disturbed intact subsurface deposits has not been publicly assessed.

Ecology

Ozark Highland; dolomite glades; dry chert woodland; rare endemic species in igneous glade communities.

Hydrology

East Fork Black River; Taum Sauk Reservoir (upstream); shut-in pools and cascades; 2005 reservoir breach reshaped lower park terrain.

Conservation Status

Missouri State Park; Missouri State Natural Areas (Johnson's Shut-Ins Natural Area, Johnson's Shut-Ins Dolomite Glade Natural Area, Johnson's Shut-Ins Fen Natural Area); Missouri Wild Areas (East Fork Wild Area, Goggins Mountain Wild Area).

Endangered Species

none documented.

Acreage

8,916 acres
