Hills north of Chillicothe, the site of a Shawnee town before American settlement began in 1790. Planning in 1972 for a 7,500-acre park shrank to under 2,000 acres after landowners objected, and the park opened with limited access in 1979.

Hocking State Forest

Proposed, pending funding
Ohio · Ohio Department of Natural Resources, Division of Forestry
Shadow historyEcology

Bought from the early twentieth century to reverse the damage of logging and poor farming, replanted by the Civilian Conservation Corps, and designated a state forest in 1949. The Hocking Honor Camp, a prison work camp, operated in the forest from the 1950s to the 1970s.

Proposed, pending funding

Energy and miningEcology

Created from American Electric Power's ReCreation Land, tens of thousands of acres of strip-mined coal land in Morgan, Muskingum, Noble, and Guernsey counties that the utility had opened to the public. The first 5,735 acres were dedicated as a state park named for Jesse Owens in 2018.

Jockey Hollow Wildlife Area

Proposed, pending funding
Ohio · Ohio Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife
3,469 acres
Energy and miningEcology

About 1,500 of its 3,469 acres in Harrison and Belmont counties were strip-mined for coal in the 1950s and 1960s; the area was established in 2004 through an agreement with Consolidation Coal, and the mined land opened to the public in 2012 after its permits were retired. In January 2026 the Oil and Gas Land Management Commission opened 1,461 acres of the Belmont County section to drilling bids.

Avi Kwa Ame National Monument

Researching
Nevada · Bureau of Land Management; National Park Service
506,814 acres · Designated March 21, 2023 · Suggested 1 time
Indigenous historyEnergy and miningEcology

Avi Kwa Ame, the Mojave name for Spirit Mountain, is the center of creation for Yuman-speaking peoples. The monument was designated in 2023 after years of tribal advocacy and closed the area to new mining and utility-scale wind development; the Crescent Peak wind project was rejected in 2018 and its successor, Kulning, was abandoned.

Camp Hale Continental Divide National Monument

Researching
Colorado · U.S. Forest Service (White River National Forest)
53,804 acres · Designated October 12, 2022 · Suggested 1 time
Shadow historyIndigenous history

Camp Hale trained the 10th Mountain Division for winter warfare during World War II. From 1958 to 1964 the CIA secretly used the camp to train about 260 Tibetan resistance fighters, a program the government acknowledged with a plaque at the site in 2010. The monument, designated in 2022 in Ute homelands, bars new mining.

Castner Range National Monument

Researching
Texas · U.S. Army (Fort Bliss)
6,672 acres · Designated March 21, 2023 · Suggested 1 time
Shadow historyEcology

A former Army artillery and training range on the Franklin Mountains above El Paso, used from World War II through Vietnam and closed in 1966. Unexploded ordnance has kept it closed to the public for decades, which also left its Chihuahuan Desert grasslands and spring poppy fields largely undisturbed; access is to open in phases as cleanup proceeds.

Chuckwalla National Monument

Researching
California · Bureau of Land Management
624,270 acres · Designated January 14, 2025 · Suggested 1 time
Indigenous historyEnergy and miningEcology

Colorado Desert lands between Joshua Tree National Park and the Colorado River, homelands of the Iviatim (Cahuilla), Chemehuevi, Quechan, and Mojave peoples. Designated in January 2025 amid pressure for utility-scale solar and critical mineral development. The White House said in March 2025 that it would rescind the designation but issued no order; a federal lawsuit now seeks to dissolve the monument, and in March 2026 the court allowed tribal nations and conservation groups to intervene in its defense.

Sáttítla Highlands National Monument

Researching
California · U.S. Forest Service (Modoc, Klamath, and Shasta-Trinity National Forests)
224,000+ acres · Designated January 14, 2025 · Suggested 1 time
Indigenous historyEnergy and miningGeologyHydrology

The Medicine Lake Highlands, a shield volcano landscape sacred to the Pit River (Achumawi) and Modoc peoples, whose porous volcanic rock feeds the region's large spring systems. The highlands were the subject of decades of litigation over federal geothermal leasing. Designated in January 2025; the White House said in March 2025 that it would rescind the designation but issued no order.
